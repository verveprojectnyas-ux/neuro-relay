# Neuro-Relay

**A communication aid for people who cannot speak or move: ALS, stroke, locked-in syndrome.**
Available in Kazakh, Russian, English and Kyrgyz.

🔗 **Open the app:** [https://verveprojectnyas-ux.github.io/neuro-relay/](https://verveprojectnyas-ux.github.io/neuro-relay/)

---

## Why it matters

People with ALS or after a severe stroke often understand everything but cannot speak or write. The easiest way for them to communicate is to pick a ready-made phrase. They can pick it in several ways:
- with a finger;
- with a single switch;
- with their eyes or a blink;
- with their thoughts, through an EEG headset.

Neuro-Relay suggests 5 short phrases that fit the conversation, helps the person pick one and says it out loud.

## Features

### 1. Talk (main page)
- The caregiver types what the other person said and picks a topic: General, Food & Drink, How I feel, Family or Rest.
- The AI suggests 5 short phrases (up to 8 words) in the selected language, using the person's profile. They appear as large numbered tiles 1–5, plus a "More phrases" tile.
- Signal buttons: **Signal 1 – Select** (blue), **Signal 2 – YES** (green), **Signal 3 – NO** (grey).
- Before speaking, the app asks "Say this out loud?". After YES, the phrase is spoken in a female or male voice (from the profile) and shown in the large "Spoken" box.
- **Patient mode (one switch):** the highlight moves over the tiles by itself, and one signal is enough: the Space key, any external switch or a blink. The scan speed adjusts to the person automatically.
- **Eye tracking:** works with an eye tracker or an ordinary webcam. Looking at a phrase for a moment selects it, and a long blink means YES. The camera is processed only on the device; no video is sent anywhere.
- **Frequent phrases:** the "↻ Often said" topic collects phrases from the history.
- **Red emergency buttons** are always on screen: "I'm in pain!", "I can't breathe!", "Call the nurse!". They are **never generated by AI**, work instantly, sound a siren and show the alarm on every open device. In "Caregiver" mode, the caregiver also gets the alarm on their own phone.
- **Session Log:** every action is saved with time, language and input method, and can be downloaded as CSV.

### 2. EEG Simulator (control by thought)
This page shows how a real EEG headset would control the app.
- **Live EEG:** an 8-channel signal streams continuously. Every second the virtual person's attention and fatigue change, which affects the strength of the P300 wave.
- **P300:** the tiles flash one by one while the person looks at the one they want. About 0.3–0.5 s later the brain responds with a P300 wave, and the neural network uses it to find the chosen tile.
- **Sentence (any words):** any sentence can be built word by word. At each step 5 word options are shown, chosen by the AI by meaning.
- **AI patient:** the same AI as the AI Assistant (Gemini or Claude) plays the person remotely. It decides what they want to say, invents the word options for the tiles and sets their attention and fatigue for every second. EEG decoding still runs in the browser.
- **Adaptation to the person:** after every confirmed choice, the neural network learns from that person's own signals.
- **Check a real recording:** upload `eeg.csv` and `events.csv`, and the network decodes them.
- Counters: selections, messages, time per message, speed in bits/min, errors caught.

### 3. AI Assistant
A chat where a caregiver can ask how to use the app.

### 4. User Profile
- **Personal data:** full name, date of birth, sex (sets the voice), condition, favourite topics and phrases, relatives.
- **Private section:** documents, hospital, phone numbers, address. Only the owner can see it, and **it is never sent to the AI**.

### 5. Volunteer Survey
- System Usability Scale (SUS): 10 statements rated 1–5, with the score calculated automatically.
- A rating of how useful the suggested phrases were, and a comment field.

## EEG neural network

| | |
|---|---|
| Data | **real recordings of 8 people with ALS**, open dataset BNCI 008-2014 (Riccio et al., 2013), 33,600 flashes |
| Channels | Fz, Cz, P3, Pz, P4, PO7, PO8, Oz · 250 Hz · 0.5–20 Hz filter |
| Architecture | multilayer network 160 → 64 → 16 → 1, trained in Python (scikit-learn) |
| Runtime | weights are embedded in the app and run in the browser; results match Python to within 0.000001 |

**Results on real patients (6×6 letter table, 10 repetitions):**
- new patient, no calibration: **77%** of letters correct (AUC 0.77);
- with per-patient calibration: **91%** (for comparison, the classic LDA method: 95%).

**In the simulator (5 tiles, 5 flash rounds; good / medium / weak signal):**
- general network: 88% / 70% / 52%;
- with adaptation, after about 10 choices: 96% / 88% / 71%.

## How to run

**Option 1: open the website.** Go to [https://verveprojectnyas-ux.github.io/neuro-relay/](https://verveprojectnyas-ux.github.io/neuro-relay/) or scan the QR code. Nothing to install; it works on phones, tablets and computers.

**Option 2: your own site on GitHub Pages.**
1. Create a public repository and upload `index.html` and this `README.md`.
2. Open **Settings → Pages**, choose **Branch: `main`, `/ (root)`** and click **Save**.
3. After 1–2 minutes the site is live at `https://YOUR-NAME.github.io/REPOSITORY/`.

**Option 3: on your own computer.** Open `index.html` in Chrome or Edge. For the camera to work, serve the file over https or localhost, for example with `python -m http.server`.

### Turning on the AI
1. Get a free Gemini key at [aistudio.google.com/apikey](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey); it starts with `AIza`. A paid Claude key (`sk-ant-…`) also works.
2. In the app, open **AI Assistant → AI key**, paste the key and click **Save and check**.

The key is stored only in your browser and is never published. Without a key the app still works, using built-in phrases instead of the AI.

> ⚠️ On the free Gemini tier, Google may use requests to improve its services. Do not enter real patient names or diagnoses.

## Safety and privacy
- The red emergency buttons do not depend on the AI or the internet.
- Documents, phone numbers and addresses from the profile are never sent to the AI.
- Camera video and EEG signals are processed only on the device.
- The app always asks YES/NO before speaking a phrase, so a mistake can be cancelled.

## Limitations
- This is a prototype for research and demonstration, **not a medical device**. It does not replace a nurse-call system: in an emergency, call for people and get medical help.
- A real EEG headset (OpenBCI, g.tec, etc.) is not connected yet. The signal is simulated, but the model is based on recordings of real patients. A headset will need synchronised flash markers, for example via Lab Streaming Layer.
- On a plain website, the log, profile and alarms are stored only on the device where the app is open.
- A Kazakh voice is not available on every device (it works best in Microsoft Edge). Browsers usually have no Kyrgyz voice, so Kazakh or Russian is used instead.

## Technologies
- A single HTML, CSS and JavaScript file: no server, nothing to install.
- Speech: Web Speech API.
- Face and eye tracking: Google MediaPipe Face Landmarker.
- Neural network: Python, scikit-learn, SciPy. Training and export live in the separate `neuro_relay_eeg` project.
- AI: Google Gemini or Anthropic Claude.
